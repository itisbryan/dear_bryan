# Streak Mechanics (reference)

Source: Mobbin, "The Psychology Behind Streaks" (study of 859 streak designs).
Core thesis: **streaks don't work by building habits — they work by weaponizing the fear of loss.** A streak stops being a record of behavior and becomes the goal itself: people protect the number rather than practice the habit. Dopamine fires in anticipation of the streak surviving, and once a count has a name, a badge, and a home-screen slot, it eclipses whatever it was counting.

## Mechanics that work

- **Identity framing.** Duolingo changed its CTA from "Continue" to "Commit to my goal" — a promise to yourself, not a momentary choice: ~10,000 more people opened the app daily. Atoms: "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."
- **Pattern completion (Zeigarnik effect).** Apple Watch rings, GitHub contribution graphs, MacroFactor — a nearly-unbroken pattern makes a missed day read as an error to fix; unfinished things stay active in the mind longer than finished ones.
- **Anthropomorphism (Tamagotchi effect).** Duolingo's Duo isn't a mascot but a character with feelings about your attendance; Finch gives you a bird that grows as you complete goals. Attachment forms when the digital thing needs you back.
- **Design for the miss.** Duolingo lifting streak freezes from one to two brought ~200,000 more people back daily; friend streaks made users 22% more likely to finish a daily lesson. Alma's 14-day "Vacation mode" pause, Paired's auto-replenishing monthly freeze, Runna rescheduling missed workouts into the week, Finch's "repair" hammer instead of a blank day — freezes and repairs retain users far better than punishment.
- **Lower the continuity bar.** Duolingo tested requiring the full daily goal vs. a single short lesson to keep a streak: 40%+ more people kept a 7-day streak with the lower bar.

## Rules of thumb

- What a streak tracks is exactly what people will optimize for: a day count, not the habit. Track the behavior you actually want (progress, quality), not just "did you open the app."
- A streak turns a count into a statement about who you are — use that, don't fight it.
- Breaking a streak rarely just resets the number; it resets the motivation.
- Streak freezes exist because the fear of missing a day was working too well.

## Warnings

- **Fear is brittle.** When a fear-based streak breaks, it takes the original motivation with it: Polivy & Herman's "what the hell" effect — missing once produces abandonment, not just guilt.
- **Overjustification effect** (Lepper, Greene & Nisbett, 1973): external rewards can replace intrinsic reasons; remove the reward and behavior drops below baseline.
- The fear isn't backed by habit science: Phillippa Lally's 12-week UCL study found missing one day "did not materially affect the habit formation process." Feeling like you failed and actually breaking the habit are not the same thing — never design missing a day as catastrophe.
- "They built the problem. Then they built the solution." Freezes fix a fear the design itself created; prefer pull mechanics (see `principles.md`) wherever possible.
