# Paywall Patterns (reference)

Sources: Mobbin, "We Studied 2,995 Paywalls. Here's What Actually Converts." + "He Tested 4,700 Paywalls. These Won." (interview with Jonathan Parra, ex-Superwall).
Core thesis: **the purchase decision usually happens before the paywall appears — a paywall is a flow, not a screen.** What separates high-converting paywalls is timing, risk reduction, value framing, and testing radically different designs. And the "right" paywall depends on the business model: quick-revenue plays and durable LTV businesses are different games.

## Tactics that work

- **Sell the outcome upstream.** Opal's onboarding sold "8 years of your life back" — quantifying life saved took trial sign-ups from 7% to 17%.
- **Multi-page beats single-page.** Information unfolds gradually ("your plan is ready" at onboarding's end) and speaks emotionally ("feeling like themselves in 4 weeks").
- **Reduce perceived risk first.** Blinkist's step-by-step trial timeline increased sign-ups, cut complaints, raised push opt-ins. Always add "no commitment, cancel anytime." Of 7/14/30-day trial tests, Headspace's 14-day trial on the annual plan won — the longer trial made the pricier plan feel less risky.
- **Clarify the offer without changing it.** Tipstop emphasized the free trial, added a discount badge, simplified wording — nearly tripled direct conversions.
- **The pay ramp.** Slopes replaced the paywall with one "redeem your free week" action (inspired by Apple's redeem flow): trial starts +25%.
- **Use friction as a filter.** Outsider required a credit card for the trial: sign-ups halved, trial-to-paid quintupled — netting 2x+ paying customers. Moonly offered trials only on the annual plan.
- **Frame the price in the user's mental models.** Tide breaks price into weekly amounts; Ahead compares the subscription to coffee or therapy. Show at most two plans; default-select yearly for LTV.
- **Exit-intent and win-back placements.** Offer the monthly plan to users balking at yearly; steep discounts are acceptable to save a churning subscriber — never as the first price a new user sees.
- **Descriptive CTA + social proof + polish.** Descriptive button copy (what the user is doing) and real reviews/ratings lift conversion; Focus Town's "Animal Crossing-esque" world-building shows polish now differentiates.
- **Experiment in the right order.** Design-format tests move the needle most (4–5 radically different variants vs. control), then personalization (name, goals, identity-mirroring imagery), price tweaks last.

## Rules of thumb

- A first paywall only needs: a headline, a couple of bullet points, and a continue button.
- Ship a simple paywall early and acquire users first; optimize only once you have real traffic. Don't optimize too early.
- Place paywalls across the lifecycle: onboarding, feature gates, settings, cancel win-back, deletion save screens.
- Pick the metric by business model: ARPU for quick launches; LTV + retention (trial-to-paid, D1/D7/D30, churn) for durable businesses. Paywall view rate 80%+ is good, 90%+ is crushing.

## Warnings

- Trial starts mean little — keeping people paying is the harder game.
- Never lead with steep discounts right after onboarding; they cannibalize revenue before users ever see full price.
- Spin-the-wheel "predetermined win" discounts and fake urgency train users to dismiss paywalls while waiting for the next offer.
- No confusing free-trial toggles — Apple began rejecting such patterns in early 2026.
- Pretty doesn't always beat ugly: an "ugly, aggressive squeeze page" once beat every polished variant. Test everything.
