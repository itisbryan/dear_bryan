# Reward Ceremony (reference)

Source: Tim Gabe, "The 3-Stage Trick Behind Every Addictive App" (~11 min).
Core thesis: **it's not what you give the user, it's how they receive it.** Most apps deliver rewards flat — a milestone triggers a plain "Congratulations" modal, a receipt. The same reward presented with ceremony is far more likely to build a habit, because dopamine (neuroscientist Kent Berridge, 1989) fires on anticipation and uncertainty, not on receipt. Turn every "receive" moment — a match, report, score, streak, earnings — from a RECEIPT into a GIFT with three stages.

## The 3 stages

1. **Anticipation.** Build uncertainty before the reveal. Apple engineered its box lid to descend over 2–4 seconds via vacuum resistance — weight, a soft whoosh, the device centered — and packaging became collectible (fan community, resale market). GameBlazers (a fantasy card-game client) reveals pack cards one at a time so each card resets the anticipation cycle: sequential delivery creates many dopamine events vs. one if shown all at once. FreeCash escalates streaks toward an animated reward chest on the final day. Rule: find a window before any result where even brief build-up changes the brain's response.
2. **Reveal / ceremony.** Give the moment visual, haptic, and audio weight. Robinhood's 2016 full-screen trade confetti and scratch-lottery free-stock reveal; FreeCash delivers earnings through an unboxing animation rather than a direct deposit — identical cost to the company, but a "felt event." Rule: the same result presented with weight is perceived as a larger reward.
3. **Celebration / afterglow.** Let the result breathe and become identity. Spotify Wrapped (200M users in 24h): the data exists year-round, but Spotify gates it, releases annually, uses sequential animated slides, and ends with a share prompt. Tinder turned two right-swipes into a full-screen explosion before the chat prompt (11 daily opens on average). Snapchat streaks: 477M daily active users, 30+ opens/day, streaks over 4,000 days — "a gift you keep earning; breaking one feels like losing something you own." Rule (Kahneman & Tversky): framing something as given rather than accessed raises perceived value. After the reveal, add a share prompt, milestone badge, or screenshot-able stat.

## Rules of thumb

- Same reward + ceremony = different brain processing → stronger habit.
- Deliver sequentially, never in bulk — each reveal should reset anticipation.
- Unwrap, don't just display (given > accessed framing).
- Gate scarce rewards (annual/limited) to run anticipation over long periods.
- End every reward moment with an afterglow artifact (shareable, ownable, streak-able) so it becomes identity, stored value, or something the user protects.
- Audit your product for "hidden gift moments" — reports, milestones, results you're already delivering flat.

## Warnings

- Ceremony is behavior design, not decoration. Robinhood's confetti was judged by Massachusetts regulators to trivialize investing and nudge frequent trading; a Jan 2024 consent order included a $7.5M fine and banned celebratory imagery tied to trading frequency. Never use ceremony to nudge harmful or high-stakes actions.
- Don't deliver only stage 2, and don't do it poorly.
- Don't copy Spotify Wrapped as a mere data summary — "Wrapped is a ceremony, not a report."

## Ceremony pre-ship checks

1. Is every key receive-moment labeled receipt or gift, with the receipts upgraded?
2. Is there an anticipation window before each reveal (even seconds)?
3. Are rewards revealed sequentially, not in bulk?
4. Does each moment close with an afterglow artifact (share prompt, badge, stat)?
5. Could the ceremony nudge a harmful or high-stakes action? (must be "no")
6. Is the ceremony serving the outcome from step 1 of the process — or just decorating?

A single fail is a ship-blocker.
